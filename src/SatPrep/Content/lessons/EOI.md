# Expression of Ideas

About **20% of the Reading and Writing section**. These questions ask you to revise writing so it is logical and meets a specific goal.

> **Big idea:** these are logic questions, not grammar questions. Every choice may be grammatically correct; only one fits the *meaning* or the *goal*.

## Rhetorical Synthesis {#EOI.SYN}

**What it tests:** a student's bulleted notes plus a **goal** ("emphasize a difference", "introduce the study to an unfamiliar audience", "present the findings"). You choose the sentence that achieves the goal.

**Strategy**
1. **Read the goal first** and underline its key words (*difference, similarity, both, purpose, result*).
2. Skip the notes at first. Check each choice against the goal only.
3. Eliminate any choice that doesn't do exactly what the goal says, even if it's accurate.
4. Only then confirm the remaining choice against the notes.

**Example.** Goal: *emphasize a difference in light observed by the two telescopes.*
Correct: "While the Hubble observes mainly visible and ultraviolet light, the Webb observes mainly infrared light."
Wrong: a sentence about only one telescope, or about launch dates.

**Traps:** true statements about the wrong feature; sentences that mention only one subject when the goal needs a comparison.

## Transitions {#EOI.TRA}

**What it tests:** choosing the word or phrase that shows the correct logical relationship between sentences.

**Strategy**
1. Cover the blank. Read the sentence **before** and the sentence **after**.
2. Name the relationship in plain words: *opposite? more of the same? because of it? an example? next step?*
3. Pick the transition that matches.

| Relationship | Transitions |
|---|---|
| Contrast | however, nevertheless, still, by contrast |
| Addition | moreover, furthermore, additionally, in addition |
| Cause / result | therefore, as a result, consequently, thus |
| Example | for example, for instance |
| Similarity | similarly, likewise |
| Sequence | then, next, later, finally |

**Traps:** "Moreover" vs. "Therefore". Ask whether the second sentence is just *another* point, or a *consequence* of the first.
