# Geometry and Trigonometry

About **15% of the Math section**. The SAT gives you a **reference sheet** with area and volume formulas, but knowing them by heart saves time.

> **Big idea:** draw and label a figure, write the formula, then substitute. Most questions are one or two steps.

## Area and volume {#GEO.AV}

| Shape | Formula |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | $A = \ell w$ |
| Triangle | $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$ |
| Circle | $A = \pi r^2$ |
| Box | $V = \ell w h$ |
| Cylinder | $V = \pi r^2 h$ |
| Cone | $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ |
| Sphere | $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$ |
| Pyramid | $V = \frac{1}{3}\ell w h$ |

**Scaling.** Multiply every length by $k$: areas multiply by $k^2$ and volumes by $k^3$.

**Worked example.** A cylinder with $r = 3$ and $h = 5$ has $V = \pi(9)(5) = 45\pi$.

**Traps:** using the diameter as the radius; forgetting the $\frac{1}{3}$ for cones and pyramids.

## Lines, angles, and triangles {#GEO.LAT}

**Key facts**
- Angles on a straight line sum to $180^\circ$. Vertical angles are equal.
- Triangle angles sum to $180^\circ$.
- Parallel lines cut by a transversal: **corresponding** and **alternate interior** angles are equal; **same-side interior** angles sum to $180^\circ$.
- **Similar triangles** have equal angles and proportional sides. Find the scale factor and multiply.

**Worked example.** $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ with $AB = 4$, $DE = 10$, $BC = 6$. Scale factor $\frac{10}{4} = 2.5$, so $EF = 6 \times 2.5 = 15$.

**Traps:** matching sides in the wrong order (use the order of the letters).

## Right triangles and trigonometry {#GEO.RTT}

**Key facts**
- Pythagorean theorem: $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. Common triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25 (and their multiples).
- **SOH-CAH-TOA:** $\sin = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}$, $\cos = \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}$, $\tan = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$.
- Complementary angles: $\sin x^\circ = \cos(90 - x)^\circ$.
- Special right triangles: $45$-$45$-$90$ has sides $1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}$, and $30$-$60$-$90$ has sides $1 : \sqrt{3} : 2$.
- Radians: $180^\circ = \pi$ radians.

**Worked example.** In a right triangle with legs 6 and 8, hypotenuse $= 10$. For the angle opposite the side of length 6, $\sin = \frac{6}{10} = \frac{3}{5}$.

**Traps:** picking "opposite" and "adjacent" from the wrong angle.

## Circles {#GEO.CIR}

**Key facts**
- Circumference $C = 2\pi r$; area $A = \pi r^2$.
- Arc length $= \frac{\theta}{360} \cdot 2\pi r$; sector area $= \frac{\theta}{360} \cdot \pi r^2$.
- Equation of a circle: $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$, with center $(h, k)$ and radius $r$.
- If the equation is expanded, **complete the square** in $x$ and in $y$.

**Worked example.** $x^2 - 6x + y^2 + 4y = 12$. Complete the square: $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 12 + 9 + 4 = 25$. Center $(3, -2)$, radius $5$.

**Traps:** giving $r^2$ as the radius; sign of the center coordinates ($(x + 2)^2$ means $h = -2$).
