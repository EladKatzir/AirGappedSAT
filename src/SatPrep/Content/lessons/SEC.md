# Standard English Conventions

About **26% of the Reading and Writing section**. These are grammar and punctuation rules, which makes this the most *learnable* domain. A handful of rules covers almost every question.

> **Big idea:** first identify the sentence's **subject**, **main verb**, and **independent clauses**. Most errors are about how these connect.

## Boundaries {#SEC.BOU}

**What it tests:** punctuation between and within clauses: periods, semicolons, commas, colons and dashes.

**The core rules**

| You are joining… | Correct | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Two independent clauses | period, **semicolon**, or comma + *and/but/so* | comma alone (*comma splice*), nothing (*run-on*) |
| Intro phrase/clause + main clause | **comma** | semicolon, period |
| Complete clause + list or explanation | **colon** | semicolon |
| Extra (nonessential) info | **matching pair**: two commas, two dashes, or two parentheses | mixing a comma and a dash |
| Subject and its verb | **nothing** | any single punctuation mark |

**Test for an independent clause:** could it stand alone as a sentence? "The library opened in 1854" — yes. "Although the library opened in 1854" — no.

**Worked example.** "Octopuses change color in under a second ______ they use this to hide." Both sides are independent, so use **second;**.

**Traps:** a comma between two complete sentences; a semicolon after an introductory phrase; opening an aside with a dash and closing it with a comma.

## Form, Structure, and Sense {#SEC.FSS}

**What it tests:** subject-verb agreement, pronoun agreement, verb tense, possessives, and modifiers.

**Subject-verb agreement.** Find the real subject by crossing out prepositional phrases. "The collection *of rare manuscripts* **is** available." The subject is *collection* (singular).

**Pronouns.** Singular collective nouns (*committee, company, team, species*) take **it / its**. Plural nouns take **they / their**. Remember: *its* is possessive and *it's* means *it is*. Likewise *their* is possessive and *they're* means *they are*.

**Verb tense.** Match time clues. "In 1928, Fleming **noticed**…" uses simple past for a specific past time. Present perfect (*has noticed*) doesn't fit a stated past date.

**Possessives**

| Meaning | Form |
|---|---|
| One bird owns wings | the **bird's** wings |
| Many birds own wings | the **birds'** wings |
| More than one bird (no ownership) | the **birds** |

**Modifiers.** An introductory description must be followed immediately by the thing it describes. "Hoping to attract visitors, **the museum** extended its hours" is correct. "…, the hours were extended" is a dangling modifier.

**Traps:** agreeing the verb with the nearest noun instead of the subject; *its/it's*; switching tense for no reason.
