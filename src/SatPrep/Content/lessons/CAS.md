# Craft and Structure

About **28% of the Reading and Writing section**: the largest domain. It tests vocabulary in context, how a text is organized and why, and how two texts relate.

> **Big idea:** think like the author. Every word and sentence was chosen to do a job.

## Words in Context {#CAS.WIC}

**What it tests:** choosing the most logical and precise word for a blank, or the meaning of a word as used in a passage.

**Strategy**
1. Cover the choices. Read the sentence and **predict your own word** for the blank.
2. Use clue words: contrast (*although, but, despite*), cause (*because, so*), and restatement (*that is, in other words*).
3. Match your prediction to the choice with the closest meaning. Check its **tone** (positive or negative) too.

**Example.** "Constant interruptions can ______ a speaker's confidence." Prediction: *weaken*, so **undermine**.

**Traps:** a word that "sounds smart" but has the wrong meaning; a word related to the topic but not to the sentence's logic. Study the **Vocabulary** flashcards to build your word bank.

## Text Structure and Purpose {#CAS.TSP}

**What it tests:** the **main purpose** of a text, the **overall structure**, or the **function** of one sentence.

**Strategy**
- **Purpose:** use a verb, as in *to describe, to argue, to explain, to challenge, to compare*. Pick the verb that fits the *whole* text.
- **Structure:** outline the passage in 2–3 steps (e.g., "common belief, then counterexamples").
- **Function of a sentence:** ask what it does for the sentences **before and after** it (introduces? gives an example? qualifies? concludes?).

**Common structures**

| Pattern | Signal words |
|---|---|
| Claim, then counterevidence | *however, yet, but* |
| Problem, then solution | *to address this, instead* |
| Sequence of steps | *first, next, then, finally* |
| Old view, then new view | *once believed, recent research* |
| Claim, evidence, limitation | *still, although, however* |

**Traps:** choices that describe only part of the text; choices with the wrong verb (e.g., "argue" for a neutral description).

## Cross-Text Connections {#CAS.CTC}

**What it tests:** how the author of one short text would respond to the other.

**Strategy**
1. Summarize **Text 1's claim** in one sentence.
2. Summarize **Text 2's position** on that same issue.
3. Decide the relationship: **disagree**, **agree**, **agree but qualify** ("yes, but…"), or **add evidence**.
4. Pick the choice that matches that relationship and uses Text 2's actual reasoning.

**Traps:** choices that make Text 2 more extreme than it is ("homework never helps") or that describe a point neither text makes.
